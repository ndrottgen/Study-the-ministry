Reading an Outline

An outline is one flow of thought, not a list of separate points. Find the governing thought, follow how each point moves it forward, and connect every point to a verse.

The title The flow Verses Sort One sentence

Start with the title

The title is the governing thought. Everything in the outline explains, proves, develops, or applies it.

  • What is the main subject, and what does the title say about it?
  • What burden does this title suggest?
  • What problem might this message be solving?
  • Why were these verses chosen?

Turn the title into a sentence. Then ask what that sentence needs to explain. That is what the Roman numerals will answer.

Follow the flow

Roman numerals are the main stages of the thought. Subpoints under them explain, prove, or apply each stage.

Ask of every Roman numeral

  1. What is this point saying?
  2. Why is it here in the outline?
  3. How does it connect to the title?
  4. How does it connect to the point before it?
  5. What burden does it advance?

Common progressions

Source→Process→Experience→Expression
Revelation→Experience→Practice
Objective truth→Subjective experience
Problem→Solution→Result
Individual experience→Corporate expression

Quick read: Roman numeral I usually introduces the source, a definition, a revelation, or a problem. The last one usually shows the experience, the result, or the expression. The burden often shows up in the title, the first point, or the last.

Connect every point to a verse

The ministry always ties points of truth to verses. That is the standard to aim for in your own study.

  • Write the reference beside each point.If the outline doesn’t give one, look for the verse the point stands on.
  • Pray over the verse.The ministry came this way: praying over the verses, receiving light, and writing down what was seen. Pray-read the verse behind each point.
  • Read the footnotes.The outlines in the Recovery Version are formed by putting the footnotes together, so the footnotes open each point. Working through them builds a bird’s-eye view.
  • Go back and forth.From the ministry to the Word, then back to the ministry. That cycle brings you into what the ministry has seen.

Sort by importance

Sift what is primary, secondary, and tertiary. Don’t assume the easiest part to understand is the crucial point.

Primary

Tied to God’s heart’s desire and His economy. If forgotten, the message is lost.

Secondary

Supports and develops the primary points.

Tertiary

Illustrations, examples, and side points.

What makes a point crucial is how it ties to God’s heart’s desire. The training’s framework for that is God’s economy: the Triune God, the Spirit for our experience, the experience of life, and its consummation in the New Jerusalem.

Watch for contrasts

law vs. Spirit self vs. Christ religion vs. life outward vs. inward knowledge vs. experience

A contrast shows what the message is correcting. Ask what spiritual condition is being exposed or healed.

Say it in one sentence

Once you see the flow, you don’t have to memorize separate points. You can rebuild the outline and speak it.
