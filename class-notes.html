Taking Notes in Class

Notes keep your being engaged and your mind active in class. They don’t need to be word for word, only enough to bring back the main points and the burden.

While listening

A whole class can often be reduced to one governing burden. If you miss it, you can have pages of notes and still miss the class.

Not only

What is he saying?

But

  • Why is he saying it?
  • What burden is behind this point?
  • What does he want us to see?

Instead of five pages of equal weight, you end up with ten or fifteen points that stand out.

Right after class

Take five minutes before you talk to anyone. Close your notes and write three sentences.

1

The burden

What was the brother trying to impart?

The burden was that the Christian life is lived by the Spirit received through faith, not by self-effort.

2

What I saw

Not what was taught, but what you saw.

I saw that walking by the Spirit is not outward behavior but living by the divine life.

3

What touched me

One sentence of your own.

I was touched that I often try to improve myself instead of turning to the Spirit.

Test: if you can’t write the burden without looking, you captured information but not yet the burden.

These three sentences are already the skeleton of a prophecy. See how to prepare one.

Later that day

About fifteen minutes turns your notes into understanding.

  1. Find the threadWhat connects everything? Write it in one sentence, then in five words or less.
  2. Find the skeletonWhat were the three to five major movements of the speaking? Ignore the details.
  3. Sort what you wroteMost notes feel overwhelming because everything is weighted the same.
    Primary
    If forgotten, the message is lost.
    Secondary
    Supports the burden.
    Tertiary
    Illustrations and extras.
  4. Pick one key verseThe verse that carries the burden.
  5. Ask whyWhy did the brother feel burdened to give this message? Why was it needed?
  6. Prepare a short prophecyIf you were asked to share right now, what would you say? Use the prophecy page.

Using your notes later

Notes are a hook. They let you pick up where you left off instead of starting from zero.

How was the message presented?

Notice the order and how the brother moved from one point to the next.

How was each verse explained?

Note how the verse was interpreted, then trace it back to the Word yourself.

Crystallize

Compress two or three sentences into one.

Keep it in one place

Store key statements together so you can browse them. See the key statement log.

Review again and again. The truth doesn’t change, but you grow. Each time you return to it, you see more.
